Project presentation · literature & contributions

scribe2tex in the research landscape

The model is a ResNet-18 encoder with Bahdanau attention and an LSTM decoder that turns an image of a math expression into LaTeX. It belongs to a well-studied line of image-to-markup papers. This page lists the papers to cite, compares the numbers honestly, and separates what was borrowed from what this project adds.

image ─► ResNet-18 (ImageNet) ─► 256-d feature grid ─► pad-masked Bahdanau attention ─► LSTM ─► beam search ─► LaTeX + confidence training: im2latex-100k (printed) ─► MathWriting 40k (handwritten) ─► HME100K (phone photos), one 429-token vocab throughout

Where it sits

  1. 2015
    Attention arrives. Bahdanau attention for translation [1]; Show, Attend and Tell applies it to images [2]. scribe2tex's decoder is this design.
  2. 2017
    Image-to-LaTeX becomes a task. Deng et al. release im2latex-100k [4]; WAP adapts the same idea to handwriting on CROHME [5].
  3. 2021–22
    Transformers and structure. BTTR [6] and CoMER [7] swap the RNN for a transformer decoder. SAN [8] adds grammar and releases HME100K.
  4. 2024
    Scale. Google's MathWriting [9] gives 230k human-written samples plus 400k synthetic ones, and benchmarks large vision-language models.
  5. 2026
    scribe2tex. Rebuilds the classic 2015–17 architecture with modern transfer learning, trains it across three domains, and ships it as a working app. pix2tex [10] stands in as the modern transformer baseline.

What was borrowed and what this project adds

Borrowed from the literature

  • Additive attention [1]
  • CNN → attention → LSTM layout, mean-feature hidden state initialisation, doubly stochastic attention loss [2]
  • Image-to-LaTeX framing, beam search, BLEU and ExpRate metrics [4, 5]
  • Pretraining on easier data, then fine-tuning on handwriting [4]
  • Padding masks for batched images [5]

Added in scribe2tex

  • ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 encoder. The 2017 baselines trained small CNNs from scratch; this gives a strong visual prior with limited handwriting data.
  • A three-domain transfer chain. Printed → handwritten → real phone photos, where Deng et al. used two stages. One shared 429-token vocabulary means each warm start keeps its output layer; it covered 99.9% of MathWriting tokens.
  • Measured gains from each stage. Handwriting token accuracy went from 0.52 to 0.82 after fine-tuning. HME100K reached 0.836.
  • Aspect ratio kept intact (height 128, padded, never squashed). Masking covers both attention and the decoder's initial state, so padding never leaks in.
  • A confidence score from beam search that falls to about 0.4–0.5 on out-of-distribution photos and sits around 0.94 on clean input. The papers report accuracy only.
  • A working product: FastAPI and Gradio over one predict() core, a Dockerfile, and iPhone HEIC support. None of the papers ship this.

How to frame it

  • Say"I rebuilt the classic attention architecture from the 2015–17 papers and modernised it with transfer learning. On printed formulas it beats the Show-Attend-Tell-style baseline from Deng et al. by about 16 BLEU points."
  • Say"I extended their two-stage pretrain-then-fine-tune idea to three domains, printed to handwritten to phone photos, and each stage gave a measurable gain."
  • Say"On handwriting I'm about 28 points behind models trained on ten times more data. Here is what I would change to close that gap."
  • Don't say"Beats state of the art on im2latex." The BLEU scripts and test subsets differ, and the exact-match numbers point the other way.
  • Don't say"52% on HME100K" without adding that it was measured on a self-carved split, not the official test set.

Next steps that would strengthen the story

  1. Fill the pix2tex row on the same 500 test images. This is the comparison the project is built around.
  2. Run full test sets and score MathWriting with its own normalisation and token-level CER, so the numbers drop straight into the paper's table.
  3. Add coverage attention from WAP [5]. It is a small change to BahdanauAttention, and the WAP paper reports about 9 points of ExpRate gain from it on CROHME 2014.
  4. Train on more MathWriting data, the full ~230k human samples plus some synthetic, to test how much of the gap is data.
  5. Evaluate on the official HME100K test set (24,607 images) if a mirror with test labels can be found.
